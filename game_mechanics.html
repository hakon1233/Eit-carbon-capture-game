1 Climate Model

Temperature Calculation

Global Temperature:

T(c) = c × 0.0105 - 3.37°C

where c is the CO2 concentration in ppm and T is the global temperature anomaly in °C.

CO2 (ppm) Temperature Status

463+1.5°CTipping Point 1

Carbon Atmosphere System

Each month, atmospheric CO2 changes based on the carbon balance between emissions and removals:

CO2 Change:

Δc = (m - 1.54) × 0.1277247

where m is net emissions in GT CO2/month. 1.54 GT/month represents Earth's natural carbon storage (18.4 GT/year), distributed between ocean absorption (12.2 GT/year) and land absorption (8.9 GT/year).

Monthly Update:

CO2new = CO2current + Δc + feedbackEffects
Component Source
Emissions Power, Industry, Transport, Buildings, Agriculture
Removals Natural sinks + player projects (reforestation, CCS)
Feedback Effects Tipping points + feedback loops

Climate Feedback Loops

Gradual amplification effects that scale linearly from start to max. Unlike tipping points, these are not permanent — reducing temperature/CO₂ reduces the effect.

Feedback Starts Max At Max Effect
Ice-Albedo +1.5°C +3.0°C +0.05 ppm/month
Ocean Saturation 450 ppm 550 ppm +0.08 ppm/month
Vegetation Stress +2.0°C +3.0°C +0.04 ppm/month

Feedback Loop Calculation

Each feedback scales linearly between start and max:

Formula: effect = maxEffect × (current − start) ÷ (max − start)

Example: Ice-Albedo at +2.25°C

  • Progress: (2.25 − 1.5) ÷ (3.0 − 1.5) = 0.75 ÷ 1.5 = 0.5
  • Effect: 0.05 × 0.5 = +0.025 ppm/month

Combined Maximum

If all 3 feedbacks are at max: +0.17 ppm/month

Regional Temperature

regionalTemp = globalTemp × regionalMultiplier

Regional multipliers account for differential warming (e.g., Arctic warms faster).

Regional Effectiveness

Each region has potential scores (0-1) that modify project costs and effects:

Adjusted Cost:

cost = baseCost × (2 - potentialScore)

Adjusted Effect:

effect = baseEffect × potentialScore

Example: Solar in Saudi Arabia (potential 0.95):

  • Cost: $10B × (2 - 0.95) = $10.5B
  • Effect: 1 ppm × 0.95 = 0.95 ppm reduction

Solar in Norway (potential 0.3):

  • Cost: $10B × (2 - 0.3) = $17B
  • Effect: 1 ppm × 0.3 = 0.3 ppm reduction

Technology Multipliers

Unlocked technologies modify base project stats:

effectiveEffect = baseEffect * potentialScore * techMultiplier
effectiveCost = baseCost * (2 - potentialScore) * techCostMultiplier

Electrification Projects

Electrification projects shift emissions from transport/buildings to the power grid, enabling deeper decarbonization.

Sector Reduction Progress

When viewing a region, the emissions breakdown shows player reduction progress with a green indicator (↓X%).

  • Multiple projects stack multiplicatively — two 15% reductions = 72% remaining (0.85 × 0.85)
  • Sector-specific floors: Power 0%, Industry/Transport 5%, Buildings 2%, Agriculture 10%
  • Reductions are permanent once applied

Event Properties

Each event has the following characteristics:

  • Duration: How many months the event lasts
  • Probability: Chance of triggering each month (e.g., 5%)
  • Effects: Income multipliers, project efficiency changes
  • Conditions: Requirements to trigger (e.g., minimum temperature)

Example: A Severe Heat Wave has a 5% monthly chance above 1.5°C, lasts 3 months, reduces income by 10% and project efficiency by 15%.

Event Processing

  1. Check if conditions are met (temperature, date, etc.)
  2. Roll probability each month
  3. If triggered, apply effects for duration
  4. Decrement duration each month until 0
  5. Remove event when expired

Volcanic Eruptions & CO2

Unlike climate disasters, volcanic eruptions are geological events that release CO2 directly into the atmosphere:

  • CO2 Emission: ~0.5 GT CO2 per eruption (+1.06 ppm)
  • Not Temperature-Dependent: Random occurrence based on regional vulnerability
  • Regions at Risk: Asia (Pacific Ring of Fire), South America (Andes), Oceania

Alliance Effects from Disasters

Climate disasters affect regions differently based on their alliance membership:

Region Status Effect Magnitude
In Alliance Happiness Boost +1 to +5 (based on severity)
Not in Alliance Interest Boost +2 to +10 (based on severity)
Neighboring (not in Alliance) Interest Boost +1 to +5 (half of direct)

Rationale: Alliance members feel validated in their climate commitment after experiencing disasters. Non-members become more interested in joining as they witness climate impacts firsthand.

Disaster Awareness

Regions that experience disasters develop increased climate awareness:

  • Awareness Boost: +3% (minor), +5% (moderate), +8% (severe)
  • Campaign Effect: Easier climate campaigns in disaster-affected regions
  • Awareness Decay: -1% per year if no new disasters (memory fades)
  • Maximum Cap: 40% awareness boost

6 Tipping Points

Irreversible Climate Thresholds

Once triggered, tipping points permanently add CO₂ each month. They cannot be reversed. Each tipping point is checked during the monthly update.

Tipping Point Thresholds

Threshold Name CO₂ Effect Additional Effects
+1.4°C Arctic Sea Ice Decline +0.08 ppm/month Reduced Earth reflectivity accelerates warming
+1.7°C Permafrost Methane Release +0.15 ppm/month Ancient methane stores released
+2.1°C Amazon Rainforest Dieback +0.12 ppm/month Forest projects −50%, Land sink −50%
+2.4°C Ocean Circulation Weakening +0.18 ppm/month Ocean absorption −30%, weather disrupted

Cumulative Effect

If all 4 tipping points trigger, the combined monthly CO₂ addition is:

0.08 + 0.15 + 0.12 + 0.18 = +0.53 ppm/month
That's +6.4 ppm/year from tipping points alone!

How Tipping Points Are Checked

  1. Each month, checkTippingPoints() runs during the game loop
  2. For each tipping point not yet triggered, checks if state.temperature ≥ threshold
  3. If triggered, the tipping point ID is added to state.tippingPointsTriggered
  4. A dramatic popup warns the player
  5. The CO₂ modifier is permanently applied each month via getTippingPointCO2Modifier()

Special Effect Details

Tipping Point Effect Mechanism
Amazon Dieback Forest effectiveness −50% getForestEffectivenessModifier() returns 0.5
Amazon Dieback Land sink −50% calculateLandAbsorption() multiplies by 0.5
Ocean Circulation Ocean absorption −30% getOceanAbsorptionModifier() returns 0.7

Election-Style Probability

Negotiation success uses an election-style system where interest represents public support for joining the coalition. The outcome includes uncertainty similar to real-world polling error.

How it works:

  1. Interest = base voter support (e.g., 40% interest = 40% support)
  2. Polling uncertainty of ±15% is applied
  3. Success if simulated election result exceeds 50%
  4. Very Low interest regions capped at 50% max success

Grievance Tracking

Regions track specific complaints that are displayed in the UI. Common grievances include:

  • High carbon tax rates
  • Low local investment percentage
  • Failed compliance terms (e.g., renewable targets)
  • Below-average CO2/GDP compared to other allies

Spontaneous Joining

Neutral regions with very high interest may spontaneously request to join the alliance.

Spontaneous Join Mechanics

  • Trigger: Interest ≥ 80 for neutral regions
  • Probability: 5% per month
  • Player Choice: Accept or Decline popup
  • If Accepted: Joins with default terms, happiness = 75
  • If Declined: Interest -15, stays neutral

Region Leaving

When a region's happiness drops to critical levels (0-19), there is a 50% chance per month that they will leave the alliance. The home region can never leave.

Domino Effect: When any region leaves, all remaining allies lose 15 happiness points due to coalition instability.

Income Gating

Income is gated by alliance status:

  • Climate Finance: Only allied regions contribute GDP-based income
  • Project Income: Only projects in allied regions generate income
  • Contribution Modifier: Allied regions may have modified contribution rates based on their GDP contribution setting

Project Building Restrictions

You can only build projects in regions that are allied with you. Attempting to build in non-allied regions will be blocked.

Full Negotiation Flow

  1. 1Check Cooldown: Verify region is not on cooldown
  2. 2Check Funds: Verify player can afford cost
  3. 3Start: Sets status to NEGOTIATING, deducts cost
  4. 4Set Terms: Player adjusts 4 term sliders
  5. 5Generate Demands: Region creates their demand list
  6. 6Submit: Roll for success based on interest and terms
  7. 7aSuccess: Status → ALLIED, terms stored, happiness = 70
  8. 7bFailure: Status → HOSTILE for 6 months

Monthly Alliance Update

Each month, the alliance system processes:

  1. Check hostile cooldowns, reset to neutral if expired
  2. Update membership duration for allied regions
  3. Update interest for neutral regions
  4. Update happiness for allied regions
  5. Check term compliance for allied regions
  6. Check equal contribution (CO2/GDP) factors
  7. Apply global temperature penalty
  8. Process regions that are leaving
  9. Check for spontaneous join requests
  10. Calculate income only from allied regions

10 Carbon Atmosphere System

10.1 System Overview

The Carbon Atmosphere System calculates the global carbon balance each month, determining how fast atmospheric CO₂ rises or falls. It runs automatically every game month during the nextMonth() function.

Core Formula

Net Emissions = Total Emissions − Total Removals
Monthly PPM Change = (Net Emissions ÷ 12) × 0.128
The factor 0.128 converts Gt CO₂/year to ppm/month (~2.3 ppm per Gt annually ÷ 12)

10.4 Sector Emission Calculation Formula

Each month, sector emissions are recalculated using this formula:

Current Emissions = Baseline × (1 + EffectiveGrowth)years × (1 − Efficiency)years × ReductionMultiplier

Where:

  • Baseline — Starting emission value for the sector (from climate-data.js)
  • EffectiveGrowth — Base growth rate × development modifier (see 10.5)
  • Efficiency — Annual efficiency improvement rate (see 10.6)
  • Years — Time elapsed since game start (monthsElapsed ÷ 12)
  • ReductionMultiplier — Player reductions from projects/policies (starts at 1.0)

Example Calculation

North America Industry after 5 years:

  • Baseline: 1.50 Gt/year
  • Base growth: 2.5%/year × 0.5 (developed) = 1.25%/year
  • Efficiency: 1.5%/year (developed)
  • No player projects yet: multiplier = 1.0

Result: 1.50 × (1.0125)5 × (0.985)5 × 1.0 = 1.50 × 1.064 × 0.927 = 1.48 Gt/year

(Slight decrease because efficiency gains outpace slow growth in developed regions)

10.8 Ocean Sink Saturation Mechanics

Ocean absorption decreases as CO₂ and temperature rise. Two penalties stack multiplicatively.

CO₂ Saturation Penalty

  • Threshold: 450 ppm
  • Penalty: −2% effectiveness per 10 ppm above threshold
  • Floor: Cannot drop below 50% effectiveness

Formula: If CO₂ > 450: penalty = ((CO₂ − 450) ÷ 10) × 0.02

Temperature Penalty

  • Threshold: 1.5°C above pre-industrial
  • Penalty: −5% effectiveness per 0.5°C above threshold
  • Floor: Cannot drop below 60% from temperature alone

Formula: If temp > 1.5: penalty = ((temp − 1.5) ÷ 0.5) × 0.05

Example Calculation

At 500 ppm and 2.5°C:

  • CO₂ penalty: (500−450)÷10 × 0.02 = 0.10 (10% reduction)
  • Temp penalty: (2.5−1.5)÷0.5 × 0.05 = 0.20 (20% reduction)
  • Combined: 10.0 × (1−0.10) × (1−0.20) = 10.0 × 0.90 × 0.80 = 7.2 Gt/year

10.9 Land Sink Saturation Mechanics

Land sinks (forests, vegetation) become stressed at high CO₂ and temperatures, and can collapse if Amazon tips.

CO₂ Saturation Penalty

  • Threshold: 500 ppm (higher than ocean's 450 ppm)
  • Penalty: −1.5% effectiveness per 10 ppm above threshold
  • Floor: Cannot drop below 50% effectiveness from CO₂ alone

Formula: If CO₂ > 500: penalty = ((CO₂ − 500) ÷ 10) × 0.015

Temperature Stress Penalty

  • Threshold: 2.0°C above pre-industrial
  • Penalty: −10% effectiveness per 0.5°C above threshold
  • Floor: Cannot drop below 30% effectiveness

Formula: If temp > 2.0: penalty = ((temp − 2.0) ÷ 0.5) × 0.10

Amazon Dieback Penalty

If Amazon tipping point triggered: −50% to land sink effectiveness

This is applied after temperature stress, so combined effects can be severe.

Example Calculation

At 3.0°C with Amazon intact:

  • Temp penalty: (3.0−2.0)÷0.5 × 0.10 = 0.20 (20% reduction)
  • Result: 3.5 × (1−0.20) = 2.8 Gt/year

At 3.0°C with Amazon collapsed:

  • Temp penalty: 20% reduction
  • Amazon penalty: 50% reduction
  • Result: 3.5 × 0.80 × 0.50 = 1.4 Gt/year

10.10 Player Reduction Multiplier

When players build sector projects, they reduce emissions via a reductionMultiplier stored on each sector. Multiple reductions stack multiplicatively.

Stacking Formula

New Multiplier = Current Multiplier × (1 − Project Reduction)

Example: Building Two Projects

  1. Start: Steel subsector multiplier = 1.0 (100% emissions)
  2. Build Green Steel (15% reduction): 1.0 × (1 − 0.15) = 0.85
  3. Build another (15% reduction): 0.85 × (1 − 0.15) = 0.7225

Total reduction: 27.75% (not 30% from simple addition)

Why Multiplicative?

This prevents players from achieving 100%+ reductions and models diminishing returns — the first reduction is easiest, subsequent ones harder.

10.12 Embodied Carbon

Building clean energy infrastructure creates temporary emissions from manufacturing materials, mining, and construction. These emissions are spread across the construction period.

Embodied Carbon Factors (Per GW of Capacity)

Technology Factor Real-World Basis
Solar PV 1.5 Mt CO₂/GW Silicon refining, panel manufacturing
Onshore Wind 0.8 Mt CO₂/GW Steel tower, concrete foundation
Offshore Wind 1.0 Mt CO₂/GW More steel, underwater cables
Nuclear 2.0 Mt CO₂/GW Massive concrete and steel requirements
Hydropower 3.0 Mt CO₂/GW Dam construction (cement-intensive)
Geothermal 1.2 Mt CO₂/GW Drilling and piping
Battery Storage 50 kt CO₂/GWh Lithium mining, cell manufacturing

Calculation Method

Monthly Embodied Rate = (Factor × Capacity) ÷ Total Construction Months × 12

Example: 10 GW solar over 18 months

  • Total embodied: 1.5 Mt × 10 = 15 Mt = 0.015 Gt
  • Monthly: 0.015 ÷ 18 = 0.00083 Gt/month
  • Annualized: 0.00083 × 12 = 0.01 Gt/year added to emissions during construction

Important: Embodied carbon disappears completely when construction finishes. It's a temporary penalty for building clean infrastructure.

10.13 Gt CO₂ to PPM Conversion

The final step converts net emissions (Gt CO₂/year) to monthly atmospheric PPM change.

Conversion Factor

EMISSIONS_TO_PPM_FACTOR = 0.128

Based on ~2.3 ppm per Gt CO₂ annually, divided by 12 months

Formula

Monthly PPM Change = (Net Emissions Gt/year ÷ 12) × 0.128

Example

  • Net emissions: 28 Gt/year
  • Monthly: 28 ÷ 12 = 2.33 Gt/month
  • PPM change: 2.33 × 0.128 = +0.30 ppm/month
  • Annual: +3.6 ppm/year

Real-World Comparison

Current real-world CO₂ increase is about 2.5 ppm/year. The game starts higher due to continued fossil fuel use, but player actions can reduce this toward zero or even negative.

12.2 Climate GDP Penalty

Regional GDP growth is reduced when global temperature exceeds +1.5°C.

Threshold: +1.5°C above pre-industrial

Penalty Rate: −10% GDP growth per °C above threshold

Formula: GDP modifier = 1 − ((temperature − 1.5) × 0.1)

Temperature GDP Growth Penalty Example Effect
≤ +1.5°C None Full growth rate
+2.0°C −5% 3% growth → 2.85% growth
+2.5°C −10% 3% growth → 2.7% growth
+3.0°C −15% 3% growth → 2.55% growth

12.3 Compounding Effect

The GDP penalty applies to growth rate, not absolute GDP. Over time, reduced growth compounds:

Example: 30 Years at +2.5°C (10% growth penalty)

  • Without penalty: $10T → $10T × 1.03³⁰ = $24.3T
  • With 10% penalty: $10T → $10T × 1.027³⁰ = $22.2T
  • Loss: $2.1 trillion (8.6% less than potential)

12.4 Combined Economic Factors

GDP growth is affected by multiple factors that stack multiplicatively:

Factor Source Range
Base Growth Rate Development level 1-5% per year
Power Grid Modifier Grid stability & pricing 0.5 - 1.0
Disaster Modifier Active disasters 0.5 - 1.0
Climate Modifier Temperature penalty 0.5 - 1.0

Effective Growth Rate = Base Rate × Power Mod × Disaster Mod × Climate Mod

12.5 Income Impact

Lower GDP means lower climate finance income (GDP × climate dedication %):

  • Base income calculation: (GDP × climatePercent ÷ 100) ÷ 12 × 1000 = $/month
  • Power grid modifier applied to income
  • Disaster modifier applied to income
  • Alliance happiness affects GDP contribution

Strategic Implication: Keeping temperature below +1.5°C protects long-term economic growth, which provides more income for climate projects.

13.2 Building Power Plants

When you build a power generation project, multiple systems are affected:

Variable Renewables (Solar, Wind, Offshore Wind)

Immediate Effects:

  • ✓ Power supply increases (+0.3 to +0.8 GW per project)
  • ✓ Demand met percentage improves
  • ✓ CO₂ emissions reduced (clean energy)
  • ✗ Grid stability decreases (variable penalty)
  • ✗ Price volatility increases without storage

Cascade: Lower stability → higher prices → GDP penalty → less income → happiness drops

Baseload Power (Nuclear, Hydro, Geothermal)

Immediate Effects:

  • ✓ Power supply increases (+0.3 to +1.2 GW per project)
  • ✓ Grid stability increases (baseload bonus)
  • ✓ Price stability improves
  • ✓ CO₂ emissions reduced (except fossil baseload)

Cascade: Higher stability → stable prices → business confidence → GDP grows → happiness increases

Fossil Fuel Plants (Coal, Gas) - "Replace" Mode

When Replacing Existing Fossil:

  • ✓ CO₂ emissions dramatically reduced
  • ✗ Power supply decreases (removing capacity)
  • ✗ May cause supply shortfall if not careful

Strategy: Build replacement clean capacity BEFORE retiring fossil plants
