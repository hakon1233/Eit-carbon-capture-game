# Norway Commercialization & Funding Strategy

Internal strategy note on adapting this climate game for Norwegian school use — who would pay for it, who would fund it, and which doors to knock on first. Blunt and practical, not a pitch deck.

---

## 1. Honest assessment: can Norwegian public education fund this?

**Short answer: not directly, and not at a scale that supports full-time development.**

- Norway does not have a central national budget that buys digital learning games for schools. Procurement is decentralized: each **kommune** (municipality) runs its own school budget, and each **fylke** (county) runs upper-secondary. That means ~350 separate buyers, each with small discretionary budgets and a strong preference for free or already-bundled tools (Feide login, existing LMS, etc.).
- **Udir** (Directorate for Education) sets curriculum and can endorse resources, but it almost never buys software itself. An Udir nod gives credibility and traffic, not revenue.
- Teachers are time-poor and risk-averse. "Free, in Norwegian, curriculum-tagged, works in Feide, 45 minutes or less" is the real bar. Anything that requires a purchase order is a hard sell.
- Realistic public-education revenue path: **per-kommune or per-fylke licenses**, typically in the 10k–150k NOK/year range each, closed one at a time over long sales cycles. You will not make a living on this alone.

Conclusion: treat the public-school channel as **distribution and credibility**, not the primary revenue engine. Fund the build through grants and foundations; use school adoption as traction evidence for the next grant round and for corporate/CSR deals.

---

## 2. Best monetization options (ranked by realism)

1. **Grant-funded free product.** Build with grant money, release free to schools. Renew grants by showing reach + learning outcomes. Highest odds of actually shipping in Norway.
2. **Freemium for schools.** Core game free; paid tier adds teacher dashboard, class reports, curriculum-linked lesson plans, offline/Feide integration. Sell the paid tier per school or per kommune.
3. **Kommune / fylke site licenses.** Annual license covering all schools in a municipality or county. Slow sales cycle but sticky once landed.
4. **Science-center and museum licensing.** Fixed-fee install at INSPIRIA, VilVite, Vitensenteret Trondheim, etc. Smaller deals (20–80k NOK) but fast-closing and reputation-building.
5. **Corporate CSR sponsorship.** A climate-exposed company (energy, finance, industry) funds a "schools edition" in exchange for branded credits and a co-signed press release. Be careful about editorial independence — see risks.
6. **International expansion later.** Once the Norwegian version works, the content is largely portable to Nordic / EU markets, which unlocks EU education funding.

Avoid: consumer app-store monetization (IAP, ads). Schools will not adopt it, and parents are not the buyer for a curriculum tool.

---

## 3. Best buyer types

Buyers = people or institutions that write a purchase order for the product.

- **Kommuner (municipalities)** — primary-school buyer. Target IT/education leads ("skolesjef", "digitaliseringsansvarlig i oppvekst").
- **Fylkeskommuner (counties)** — upper-secondary buyer. Larger budgets, fewer decision makers than kommuner.
- **Individual schools** — only realistic for small pilots or freemium upgrades; they rarely have real procurement authority.
- **Science centers (Vitensentrene)** — INSPIRIA (Sarpsborg), VilVite (Bergen), Vitensenteret (Trondheim), Vitenfabrikken (Sandnes), Nordnorsk vitensenter (Tromsø). They buy exhibit content and workshop tools.
- **Naturfagsenteret (National Centre for Science Education)** — not usually a direct buyer, but can commission or co-develop resources and route budget through Udir-adjacent programs.
- **Private/international schools in Norway** — small market but faster procurement, willing to pay in EUR/USD.

---

## 4. Best funder types

Funders = entities that give non-dilutive money to build/operate, not to own the product.

- **Innovasjon Norge** — grants and soft loans for Norwegian startups with export potential. Relevant programs: Oppstartfinansiering, Innovasjonskontrakter, Markedsavklaring. Expects commercial intent and matching funds.
- **Forskningsrådet (Research Council of Norway)** — larger research-linked grants. Relevant calls: Innovasjonsprosjekt i næringslivet (IPN), FORREGION, and education-research calls tied to learning outcomes. Needs an academic or R&D partner.
- **Sparebankstiftelsen DNB** — one of the strongest philanthropic funders in Norway for children, learning, and culture. Typical grants 100k–2M NOK, sometimes larger. Loves projects that are free to end users.
- **Gjensidigestiftelsen** — broad social-purpose grants; climate education and youth well-being both fit. Annual application windows.
- **Other savings-bank foundations (sparebankstiftelser)** — Sparebanken Vest, SpareBank 1 Stiftelsen, etc. Regional bias — useful when targeting a specific kommune or fylke.
- **EU funding (Erasmus+, Horizon Europe, Digital Europe)** — real money but bureaucratic and needs 2–3 international partners. Realistic only after a Norwegian MVP exists.
- **Municipal/county innovation funds** — some kommuner and fylker (e.g. Oslo, Viken successor regions, Vestland) have small climate or digitalization pots.

---

## 5. Credibility partners (not buyers, not funders)

These don't pay you, but their logo/endorsement changes the funding and sales conversation. Line them up early.

- **Naturfagsenteret** — curriculum authority for natural sciences. An endorsement or co-authored lesson plan is gold for school sales.
- **Udir** — listing on resource portals (e.g. udir.no, Tekna/NDLA adjacent channels) drives teacher awareness.
- **Universities / teacher-education programs** (UiO, NTNU, OsloMet, UiB) — research partners for Forskningsrådet applications and for measuring learning outcomes.
- **NGOs / climate partners** — WWF Norge, Zero, Bellona, Miljøagentene, Regnskogfondet, Framtiden i våre hender. They provide subject-matter credibility and distribution to their networks. Some also run small grant programs.
- **Miljødirektoratet / Klima- og miljødepartementet** — occasional project funding for climate awareness, and useful credibility if they endorse.

## 6. Ranked shortlist: strongest Norway-relevant targets to approach first

Ranked by fit × realism × speed-to-decision for an early-stage school climate game.

1. **Sparebankstiftelsen DNB** — best single bet. Fits their "barn og unge" + "kunnskap og læring" themes, appreciates free-to-end-user products, writes grants large enough to fund a real build. **Ask: project grant, 500k–1.5M NOK.**
2. **Innovasjon Norge (Oppstart / Markedsavklaring)** — matches a startup doing validation with commercial potential. Useful even if the first product is free, as long as there is a credible monetization roadmap. **Ask: Markedsavklaring grant (~100k NOK) first, then Kommersialiseringstilskudd.**
3. **Vitensentrene (INSPIRIA first, then VilVite, then Vitensenteret Trondheim)** — fastest path to real users, small paid deals, and photo-op credibility. **Ask: pilot install + paid annual license.**
4. **Naturfagsenteret** — co-development or endorsement. Unlocks teacher trust. **Ask: curriculum review + co-branded lesson plan, possibly a small commissioned work.**
5. **Gjensidigestiftelsen** — second foundation grant, complementary to Sparebankstiftelsen DNB. **Ask: project grant, 200k–800k NOK.**
6. **A pilot kommune** — Oslo, Bergen, Trondheim, or a smaller climate-forward one (e.g. Asker, Bodø, Stavanger). Start with one that already publicly prioritizes climate education. **Ask: free 1-year pilot across 3–5 schools in exchange for a case study and usage data.**
7. **Forskningsrådet IPN with a university partner** — bigger money, longer horizon. **Ask: 2–3 year research-and-innovation grant, co-applied with a university learning-sciences group.**
8. **NGO partnership (WWF Norge or Miljøagentene)** — co-branding and distribution to their youth networks. **Ask: endorsement + joint launch, possibly a small co-funded module.**
9. **CSR sponsor** — target one company whose climate story genuinely aligns (e.g. Statkraft, Equinor's "young talent" programs, Storebrand, DNB, Hydro, Yara). **Ask: "schools edition" sponsorship, 300k–1M NOK, with clear editorial independence.**
10. **Fylkeskommune license deal** — pursue after 1–2 school case studies exist. **Ask: annual license for all upper-secondary schools in the county.**

## 8. Recommended first go-to-market path

A 12-month sequence that stacks credibility and cash in the right order.

1. **Months 0–2. Lock one credibility partner and one pilot site.**
   - Get Naturfagsenteret (or a university learning-sciences group) to sign off on curriculum alignment — even informally.
   - Land a free pilot at one science center (INSPIRIA is most approachable) and/or 2–3 schools in one climate-forward kommune.
2. **Months 2–4. Apply to Sparebankstiftelsen DNB + Innovasjon Norge Markedsavklaring in parallel.**
   - Use the pilot and the endorsement to make both applications land harder.
3. **Months 4–8. Ship a polished free version in Norwegian, with Feide login and a teacher dashboard.**
   - Measure usage and learning outcomes. Publish a short impact note.
4. **Months 6–10. Add a second foundation (Gjensidigestiftelsen) and approach one CSR sponsor.** Use the free version's traction as proof.
5. **Months 8–12. Convert pilots into paid kommune/fylke licenses.** Start with the pilot kommune — easiest first sale. Package the freemium tier around teacher features, not core gameplay.
6. **Months 10–12+. Begin a Forskningsrådet IPN application with a university partner,** aimed at a 2–3 year extension and Nordic/EU expansion.

Do **not** start by cold-emailing kommuner, and do **not** start by building a payment system. Start with one foundation grant and one credible pilot site — everything else follows from those.

---

## 9. Risks and blunt caveats

- **Public-school procurement is slow and small.** Plan for long sales cycles and modest ticket sizes. If your business model needs fast school revenue, it will not work in Norway.
- **Free is the default expectation.** Once teachers see a free version, upgrading them to paid is hard. Design the free/paid split carefully from day one — the paid layer should be genuinely for the school/kommune, not the student.
- **Curriculum alignment is not optional.** Without LK20 tags and a Norwegian-language version, most teachers will not adopt. Translation and curriculum mapping is real work, not a weekend task.
- **Privacy / GDPR / Feide.** Anything that looks like student data leaving Norway is a deal-breaker. Budget for privacy review before any pilot.
- **CSR sponsorship has reputational risk.** Taking money from an oil major for a climate game will be used against you by NGOs and press. If you go this route, get editorial independence in writing and be ready to defend the choice publicly.
- **Foundations don't fund forever.** Sparebankstiftelsen DNB and Gjensidigestiftelsen typically fund projects, not operations. Have a plan for year 2 revenue before year 1 grant money runs out.
- **Grant-writing is a real cost.** A serious Forskningsrådet application can take 200+ hours. Don't apply to everything — pick 2–3 per year and do them well.
- **Founder-market fit matters here.** Norwegian education buyers strongly prefer dealing with a Norwegian-registered AS with someone who can show up in person. If the team is fully remote/abroad, find a Norwegian co-lead or board member early.
- **Beware "interest" vs "commitment".** Municipalities will happily pilot for free forever. Set a clear conversion date in the pilot agreement, or the pilot becomes the product.

---

## 10. One-line summary

Fund the build with **Sparebankstiftelsen DNB + Innovasjon Norge**, get credibility from **Naturfagsenteret + a science center**, earn first real money from **kommune/fylke licenses and science-center deals**, and treat **CSR + Forskningsrådet + EU** as the year-2 expansion lane.
